## 2. Alternatives and why

1. **A faithful port of the family-probe path** (chosen) vs. a redesign (a pipe or a file descriptor instead of stdin; a one-shot token file). The brief names the family-probe path as the design and the user's 7 Oct decision describes it ("It reaches a pod only through the ssh-stdin gated-download step: never in a file, env var or log; deleted after use", research/FOCUS.md:472-475, R). The port keeps every reviewed fix of 8 Oct (the whole whoami reply read, an unreadable reply only warns, the redirect handler, the scrub after use, one record per attempt). Two things are new: `check_hosts` refuses another family's plan (KD7), and `get`/`runner` are resolved at the call (section 7 item 2).
2. **An unreadable or oddly shaped whoami reply warns, the Range check decides** (chosen; the family-probe's review fix 6) vs. refusing. The brief's test list says "whoami refusal paths (HTTP error, unreadable reply, wrong user shape)". I kept the family-probe semantics: an HTTP status other than 200 refuses; a 200 whose body is not JSON, not the user shape (a list, `auth` a string, `accessToken` a list, no `auth`) gives role "unknown (whoami-v2 reply unreadable)" or None and a `token-scope-warning`, and the ten Range requests must still all answer 206, else the launch refuses. Reason: the user waived the read-only precondition, so the role cannot be a refusal reason, and the Range check is the decisive access test. Section 7 item 1 gives the lead the two-line tightening if a refusal is wanted.
3. **`HF_TOKEN_ENV = rules.TOKEN_ENV_NAMES[0]`** in launch.py and orchestrate.py (chosen) vs. a new constant in `stage1_rules.py` (K1's file). The first scrubbed name is the one read; a test pins it to "HF_TOKEN" in both modules; no edit to K1's module.
4. **`gated_entries()` from `probes.stage1_download_plan(rules.PROBE_KEY)`** in both modules (chosen) vs. a flag in `rules_78b.KIT` (`instruct_model.gated` exists there). The download plan is what the pod downloads and what the Range check must cover (the ten exact files), so the gate and the check read the same list; `rules_78b.KIT[*]["instruct_model"]` repeats it and K1's test holds them equal.
5. **The whole environment for the detached orchestrate child of a gated family** (chosen; the family-probe design) vs. passing the token to orchestrate on stdin too. orchestrate is a detached, long-lived child (caffeinate); its stdin is not a channel the kit uses. The window in which orchestrate holds the token is from its start to the gated step (after bootstrap-ready: E, 10-20 min); every child it starts in that window runs under `clean_env`. An open family's child is scrubbed (a mutant holds it).
6. **The two block-B judge-route tests removed, with a pin** (chosen) vs. skipped vs. left failing. They test 1B block-B records through the kit copy's plan_tool (`judge_files_check(plan, "B", …)`, `record_judges("B", …)`), which this copy refuses by design (`BLOCKS == ("0",)`); the judge route's own files and its nine other tests are untouched. A skip would hide the reason in a marker; K2's one-test-pin form is the kit's convention.
7. **The guard-off, extend-cap, $10/h, kit-folders, full-cap and K6 tests ported** into test_gated_78b.py (chosen) vs. leaving them unported (K1's report section 8 left "launch/host/extend-cap/guard (8, the launch path)" to K3). They cover my modules; on block 0 the numbers change (8.5 h x $1.85 = $15.725; the pod name `attribution-s78-…`).
8. **Mutants over test_gated_78b.py alone** (34, one 2.3-minute foreground run) vs. the whole suite per mutant (about 20 s each, 12 min). K2's and T2's specs stay their own; the brief's 10-minute foreground limit is met.
