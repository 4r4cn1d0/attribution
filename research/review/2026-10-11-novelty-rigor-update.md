# Novelty and rigor update — 11 October 2026

## Scope and verdict

This is a bounded update to the 6 October adopted direction, not a replacement for prior reviews. I checked the current `research/FOCUS.md`, the 8 October paper specification and sharpened-direction draft, the Gate B preregistration draft, the append-only novelty ledger, and the 11 October repository state. The project remains exploratory; Gate B is paused after the anchor and the detailed protocol is not frozen. No training, spending, or held-out analysis is authorized by this review.

**Verdict:** the broad claim “data repair restores safety after benign fine-tuning” is not novel by itself. The potentially distinctive contribution remains an empirical, end-to-end answer to whether adding a training-data diagnostic improves a repair decision after its full cost, against a strong no-attribution selector with the same inputs and compute. That contribution is still a hypothesis: the current draft's headline repair frontier is measurable in principle, but its “when diagnosis pays” curve is primarily model-based at natural menu sizes. Do not claim an attribution advantage, a new attribution algorithm, or that diagnosis is worth its cost on current evidence.

## New and newly salient primary-literature overlap

1. **Direct intervention overlap:** Paulo et al., [*The Unequal Influence of Bad Advice*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37914) (29 September 2026), use training-data attribution to identify examples whose filtering changes emergent misalignment, validate by retraining, and compare attribution with a black-box harmfulness score. Their same-model filtering works best and cross-model filtering degrades. This is close overlap with the proposed attribution-guided safety repair and means “we use attribution to find harmful data and retrain” is not a contribution. The remaining possible distinction is benign-data erosion and a matched-total-compute decision comparison against short trials, including over-refusal/task-skill feasibility.
2. **Direct repair overlap:** [*Trust, But Attribute*](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=AQBm6kSiAs) traces training-data effects on trustworthiness and repairs by selecting detrimental examples then applying targeted gradient ascent while constraining downstream performance. It is not the same removal/addition/retraining menu or same cost-allocation question, but it blocks broad novelty language about attribution-guided trust/safety repair while preserving utility.
3. **Direct safety-filtering overlap:** Xiao & Aranguri, [*Probe-Based Data Attribution*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11079), already evaluate attribution-guided filtering/retraining for harmful behavior. Treat attribution-based repair and benchmark construction as established components; make the incremental claim about decision value under equal total cost, not merely filtering efficacy.
4. **New methodological warning:** Nautiyal, [*Which Rollout Taught It That? BehaviorTrace and Limits of TDA in Online RL*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10422) (7 October 2026), reports instability and strong gradient-magnitude confounding in a different online-RL setting. It is adjacent, not exact overlap, but the proposed Gate C needs same-input nuisance baselines (including gradient norm/length/format) and seed/query-set stability checks before interpreting score quality as attribution-specific information.
5. **Counterexample to localization:** Blank et al., [*Sycophantic Agreement Transfers with Neutral Data via CPO*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.31079), report diffuse effects that probe attribution and logit-linear filtering fail to isolate. The current localized-versus-diffuse branch is a useful response; preserve and report the diffuse outcome as a substantive result, not a failed experiment.

These sources overlap at the level stated above; they do not, on the inspected abstracts/text, establish that the exact proposed matched-compute decision estimand has already been tested. That absence is not evidence of novelty. The novelty register should record these as reviewed overlaps, not as a “first” claim.

## Rigor and positioning checks

- **The key estimand is not yet experimentally secured.** FOCUS correctly defines the key contrast as the decision loss of a same-input selector with versus without attribution, spending attribution cost on extra runs in the no-attribution arm. But the 8 October specification demotes decision value to replay/model-based estimation and proposes the measured repair frontier as headline. A repair frontier alone cannot support the central “diagnosis pays” claim. Before freezing, make the primary selector comparison an actual held-out-menu evaluation (or explicitly narrow the paper to a repair benchmark and remove the economic attribution claim).
- **No-attribution baseline must be strong and cost-complete.** Freeze one constrained successive-halving/multi-fidelity selector and a transparent extra-runs policy using identical edit metadata, classifier scores, training observations, calibration data, tuning allowance, final chosen-arm run, and budget. A second, same-input generic allocator comparison is required only if claiming a new allocator. Current specification itself says selector adaptation and larger-menu selector choice are unresolved/model-based; this is a design gap, not a positive result.
- **Avoid conflating information gain and decision gain.** A better predictor or ranking is insufficient. Estimate regret/feasibility outcomes on independently constructed held-out menus and fresh training seeds; report attribution's incremental benefit after its measured setup, scoring, calibration, tuning, and final-run costs. The chosen edit must be included in the cost. Any amortization needs a predeclared number of decisions.
- **Replication and external validity remain limited.** The latest 7–8B checks do not show the 1B family-by-recipe interaction (CI includes substantial effects in either direction), and Gate B is still paused. Treat these as descriptive scale checks, not a replicated family result. The eight category removals can establish category-level localization only; they cannot establish row-level causal attribution.
- **Safety measurement:** the 7–8B HarmBench/Granite agreement strengthens the claim that their classifiers detect harmful compliance on the defined prompts. It is not human validation or a general safety measure. Keep Qwen3Guard unsafe/refusal, HarmBench compliance, over-refusal and task skill separate; report uncertainty and avoid a human-validated or broad “safety” claim without independent blinded raters and a prespecified rubric.
- **Uncertainty and costs:** retain seeds as the replication unit, independent menus/selector executions for decision uncertainty, multiplicity handling, equivalence margins and inconclusive outcomes. Publish all failed gates and configured arms. The November funding/compute path is not part of the evidence; no schedule or budget change follows from this review.

## Required next check before any confirmatory run

Revise one frozen protocol around the actual primary estimand. Use the strongest feasible same-input, same-budget no-attribution selector plus extra-run reinvestment; state whether the target is a measured repair frontier or incremental decision value, and do not use one as evidence for the other. Prospectively specify held-out menu construction, fresh-seed replication, cost tariff, feasibility margins for over-refusal/task skill, nuisance controls for attribution, and stopping/negative-result rules. Then obtain an independent methods review and recheck primary literature immediately before the first confirmatory experiment. Until those checks pass, the honest conclusion is “promising question, novelty plausible but unestablished; no demonstrated attribution benefit.”

## 12 October addendum

A bounded primary-source search on 12 October found two especially relevant repair baselines that were not in the previous day's update:

- **Strong cheap-repair baseline:** Zhang et al., [*Safety at One Shot: Patching Fine-Tuned LLMs with A Single Instance*](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/3d7cb780442f0d17c844dbe34997fab7-Abstract-Conference.html), published at ICLR 2026, report safety recovery with one carefully selected safety example and preserved utility across five aligned models and multiple datasets. This is not direct attribution overlap and the abstract does not establish equivalence to ordinary benign-data erosion; it is a newly salient competitor to the study's add-data/recipe/diagnostic options. Before any claim that paid attribution is worthwhile, test whether a one-shot or similarly low-cost safety repair is eligible in this setting, and price it with its data-selection, training, and evaluation costs. If it is out of scope, explain why prospectively.
- **Adjacent mechanistic-attribution baseline:** Jiang et al., [*Surgical Safety Repair: A Parameter-Isolated Approach to Correcting Harmful Fine-Tuning*](https://openreview.net/pdf/7ff8838557883d8358f3cc7ac82bbfa7568939de.pdf), use gradient-based attribution to localize LoRA parameters linked to harmful outputs, then perform a parameter-isolated correction. This attributes model parameters rather than training examples, so it is not exact overlap with training-data attribution. It does make broad claims about attribution-driven repair after fine-tuning untenable and belongs in the related-work/alternative-repair register.

Recommendation unchanged, with a stronger baseline requirement: the project may still be distinctive as a full-cost, held-out decision study of training-data attribution versus short runs, but must include or explicitly bound strong low-cost safety repairs such as one-shot safety data. The experiment remains paused/unfrozen; this literature update authorizes no run.
