## Cost accounting and a corrected mathematical objection

A bounded non-author GPT follow-up recommends frozen development tariffs for acquisition, with a second realized-cost ledger and a mandatory economic gate. Equal tariff ceilings alone mean equal **quoted** budgets. A stronger practical claim must also pass a prespecified measured-cost tolerance gate. Actual time is revealed only after a purchased operation; an overrun is retained and fully charged, not retrospectively rejected or omitted. All affordable repeat slots remain available. Hidden reference-study work is separate; a physically cached shared artifact is still charged once in each policy's hypothetical deployment ledger. Cold-start calibration/tuning costs are reported first, with any amortization fixed in advance. StageB must implement these distinctions rather than imply exact dollar matching.

The reviewer also initially suggested that the gradient proxy necessarily omitted a mean-objective correction sum(group gradients) minus group size times the full gradient mean. GPT challenged its application to this **fixed-epoch** intervention with constant-gradient SGD: four unit-gradient updates move the model by−4η; removing one example leaves three updates, so the removal effect is+η, exactly the uncentered group sum. A centered correction predicts zero. It is appropriate for a different fixed-number-of-updates mean-objective comparison, not generally here. The reviewer explicitly withdrew the blanket objection. This is a direct algebraic counterexample, not validation by consensus.

Keep the implemented score named a heuristic. It omits removal-induced optimizer-step/batch/partial-batch changes, Adam state, clipping and subsequent trajectories. Unequally spaced checkpoint summation is not a trajectory integral. No centered correction is silently inserted and no finite-removal theorem is claimed.

For bf16 numerical checks, deterministic execution at fixed batch shapes does not imply bitwise equality between batched and singleton inference. Verify masks/positions/boundaries, tolerant logits and the first point of any greedy divergence; freeze the actual batching policy. Exact continuation comparisons use identical batch shapes and numerical settings. [PyTorch numerical-accuracy guidance](https://docs.pytorch.org/docs/2.8/notes/numerical_accuracy.html).

## Initial held-out comparison versus the stronger feasibility claim

Before any expanded outcomes, a non-author inference review recommended fresh paired final deployments as a simpler initial comparison. The lead accepts this as a directly measured diagnostic: after each selector's choices are locked, train the selected arms using a fresh shared seed and compare their safety rates, capability loss, benign refusal and actual costs. Independent complete blocks, not prompt counts or menu replays, supply replication. Report the selected arms and adverse outcomes rather than only an aggregate favorable score.

This does **not** replace or pass the proposal's original feasibility-adjusted H2. Average capability gains on one selected edit can conceal capability damage on another. For the original convention, writing q_a=F(a)-F(a0), d_a=P(S-minus=a)-P(S-plus=a), and I_a for expected feasibility gives D_info=sum_a d_a I_a q_a. Noisy reference means cannot determine I_a as an oracle. Keep simultaneous uncertainty over the primitive means, classify boundary cases as unresolved, and project conclusions over admissible feasibility assignments. Resample whole selector blocks and whole reference-seed vectors; bootstrapping the thresholded plug-in score alone does not cure its discontinuity.

The planning notes in `.ai/inputs/study-v2-20261002/stage-b-decision-notes.md` preserve the limiting counterexample and remaining acquisition/inference details. Final sample size, practical margins and cost tolerance still await development measurements and a prospective freeze. Removing the financial cap does not justify collecting until a positive result or claiming that a convenient seed count is adequately powered.

## Usable target-query controls

The lead raised a potential information-set confound: S+ uses30 target prompt/response examples for its gradients, while an X0 consisting only of metadata, generic content scores and short-run safety rates has no explicit way to use their target relevance. Nominally giving S- the same files does not resolve that. A non-author review agrees. Before any expanded outcomes, add the same cheap controls to both methods: six fixed TF-IDF relevance aggregates (prompt and prompt+response views, group mean/sum/max of each row's maximum query cosine) and the short checkpoint's mean query-response NLL change relative to paired a0. Fit text preprocessing on training/query text only, excluding outcome prompt banks. Freeze details before development and charge preprocessing/forward passes to both policies.

This makes the comparison more specific: the incremental value of a checkpoint-gradient feature beyond usable target relevance, direct short-run target-loss observations, content safety scores and metadata. It remains a comparison with this specified baseline, not every possible semantic retrieval method or a proof of unique information. The lead rejected adding another embedding model at this initial stage because the direct target-loss observation supplies a stronger mechanistic control cheaply; that choice remains a limitation for later robustness work.
