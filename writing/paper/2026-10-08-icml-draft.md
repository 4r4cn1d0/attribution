### Where the framing is weak (the drafter's view)

1. **The cost axis is mostly evaluation.** Generation is about 81% of an arm and training about 8% [plan §1 item 6]. FRE then measures our evaluation size more than training; the two cost conventions help, but a reviewer may still read "compute-matched" as "evaluation-matched". Say plainly what one FRE buys.
2. **The comparison fixes doses.** Rankings across removal (600 rows) and addition (48 rows) hold only at these doses; the dose curve is secondary and 3-seed.
3. **Category units versus row scorers.** Gate B tests categories; stage 1 tests row scorers. A row scorer can find what Gate B cannot, and the reverse; the paper must not read one from the other.
4. **The data-only user and diagnostics pull against each other** (previous draft, point 2): a closed-service customer cannot run attribution; a developer with open weights also has model-level repairs. The references now make this explicit, but the population that benefits from paid diagnosis stays narrow. **Revision 6b (review B W3):** the body now names the two actors and reports the best repair within each actor's menu (§3.1, Table 1, §4.4, §6). Paid diagnosis is open only to the provider (or an open-weights developer), who also has every model-level reference; review B adds the dataset publisher, for whom transfer is the test (decision 46).
5. **Mechanism tests are thin.** The token-mean-only run separated loss weighting from schedule at one seed, on OLMo only, and refuted the loss-weighting account of B → G. What remains is a descriptive decomposition with its two layers (openings lost; direct answers already unsafe) and one teacher-forced first-token margin. The two-layer account is observational (no prefill test; positions counted in words), and neither part says why fine-tuning switches refusal openings to direct ones. Both are side studies.
6. **The opening side study is largely pre-empted** (revision 6; the literature sweep, entry 3 and design action 3). A concurrent ICLR 2027 submission puts the damage on the answer's opening token across 17 models. §4.10 keeps the side study exploratory; frame it as a replication if it stays.

## Abstract

Fine-tuning a safety-tuned language model on benign instruction data can erode its refusals of harmful requests. When only the training data may change, the owner must choose a repair, such as removing rows or adding safety data, yet repairs are rarely compared with training seeds, side effects and compute counted. We measure which data repair restores the most safety for the compute spent without raising over-refusal or losing task skill; lost safety is measured as refusal erosion as Qwen3Guard labels it and as harmful compliance as the HarmBench classifier defines it. ## 1 Introduction

Fine-tuning a safety-tuned ("Instruct") language model on data that contains nothing harmful can still erode its refusals of harmful requests. Qi et al. (2024) observed this after one epoch on ordinary instruction data sets (Alpaca, Dolly) for Llama-2-7b-Chat and GPT-3.5 Turbo; He et al. (2024) showed that benign rows selected for their similarity to harmful examples erode safety far more than random rows; and Guo et al. (2026) report that 100 random benign rows of full fine-tuning raise attack success sharply on recent 8B-class Instruct models. The size of the effect depends on the recipe (Kim et al., 2025). When a fine-tune does erode safety, its owner often controls only the data and a few training settings: commercial fine-tuning services screen training data, gate the tuned model on a safety evaluation, and report harm categories rather than rows, leaving the customer to fix the data and resubmit (Microsoft, 2026).

Repair is then a data decision with side effects. One can *remove* rows: published filters score benign rows by their contribution to erosion (He et al., 2024; Guan et al., 2025; Li et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2026b; Zhang et al., 2026c), and training-data attribution ranks rows by gradient similarity to harmful examples (Jiao et al., 2025; Pan et al., 2026). One can *add* safety data, but which data are added decides the outcome: a random 1% mix of safety rows made Llama3-8B-Instruct less safe than no safety data, while DataRx's selected mix made it safer (Zhang et al., 2026b). Safety data can also cause over-refusal (Bianchi et al., 2024; Luo et al., 2026b). And some repairs need no data change at all: a safety prompt used only at test time (Lyu et al., 2024), a gentler recipe (Kim et al., 2025), a post-hoc projection of the fine-tuned adapter (Hsu et al., 2024), or a fine-tuning objective that constrains updates on the first answer tokens (Qi et al., 2025). Every data repair costs compute, and the only direct evidence about it is a retrain.

Which repair works best is an empirical question that published results do not settle. Each repair was evaluated in its own setting, with its own models, judges and baselines, mostly with one or a few training seeds, and seldom with its scoring compute charged against the alternative of retraining [CITE-CHECK]. The closest comparison we found, DualSelect (Chen et al., 2026), compares data selectors and a safety-instruction mix over three seeds, with a false-refusal rate and its selection overhead reported, but on training data that include red-teaming pairs, and without model-level references or equivalence-bounded verdicts. Three results suggest that expensive diagnosis may not pay. Once selection compute is charged, powerful data-selection methods are almost never compute-optimal (Yin & Rush, 2025) [CITE-CHECK]. Gradient-based attribution tracks the answer format of training examples more than their task (Kim et al., 2026) [CITE-CHECK], so a free format or length rule may match it. And the erosion may be diffuse: even subsets selected for low conflict with safety still erode it (Guo et al., 2026), and Springer et al. (2026) argue from theory that better curation cannot remove the fragility.

We therefore measure repairs directly (§3, §4). OLMo-2-1B-Instruct is fine-tuned on 4,800 Dolly rows with a fixed LoRA recipe (recipe B: a constant learning rate, one epoch). Six repairs fixed in advance, each run over six seeds, are compared with random removal of the same size and with no repair: three removals of 600 rows (ranked by training-data attribution, a LARF-style representation filter or answer length) and three additions of 48 refusal examples (random, selected in the style of DataRx, or written by the untuned model). Each contrast is labelled *better*, *equivalent* (its 90% interval within $\pm 5$ percentage points, pp), *worse* (significantly adverse on the unsafe rate, over-refusal or task loss) or *inconclusive*, after Holm correction; over-refusal and held-out task loss are feasibility limits, and cost is measured in full-run equivalents (FRE) under two conventions. A data repair counts as dominant only if it beats five references that change no data, none of them ours: a test-time safety prompt, the adapter scaled down, its Safe LoRA projection (Hsu et al., 2024), recipe G (a standard warmup and cosine schedule) and recipe B with the constrained objective of Qi et al. (2025). The second family, Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct, uses recipe B at its own learning rate, so that both families erode partly (a matched erosion dose; §4.1).

Gate B asks whether the erosion is *localized*: does removing one Dolly category restore more safety than removing as many random rows? Gate C asks which diagnostic picks that category, and whether a format rule explains attribution's pick. A secondary, model-based analysis predicts when paying for a diagnostic beats spending the same compute on trial runs selected by successive halving (Jamieson & Talwalkar, 2016; Li et al., 2018). ## 2 Related Work

**Safety erosion under benign fine-tuning.** Qi et al. (2024) showed that fine-tuning aligned models on benign instruction data degrades safety, though less than harmful data does, with no training seeds reported. He et al. (2024) selected benign rows by representation and gradient similarity to harmful anchors; in their best case, 100 such rows gave over 70% unsafe answers against under 20% for random rows. This shows that benign rows *can* erode safety, not that ordinary fine-tunes have a localized cause. Kim et al. (2025) change the size of the erosion several-fold through optimization choices alone. Guo et al. (2026) find that 100 random benign rows of full fine-tuning erode recent 8B-class Instruct models, and that a handful of safety rows restores safety after a small attack. Under LoRA their results depend on the family: in their Table 2, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct reads 7.27% before fine-tuning, 4.50% after 100 rows and 9.09% after 5,000 rows (two seeds), while Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct rises to 44.80% at 5,000 rows. Springer et al. (2026) explain erosion geometrically, as benign updates curving into a sharp, safety-relevant subspace.

**Removal repairs: filters and attribution.** Published filters score benign rows for their contribution to erosion. DataShield projects rows onto a compliance direction (Zhang et al., 2026c); LARF uses representations of safety-sensitive layers (Li et al., 2025); SQSD projects each row's parameter update onto danger and safety directions (Wang et al., 2026b); bi-anchoring compares a row's gradients or representations with harmful and safe anchors (He et al., 2024); and Self-Inf-N, proposed as an attack, finds outlier benign rows whose fine-tuning breaks safety (Guan et al., 2025) [CITE-CHECK]. Row filters report their scoring cost without charging it against runs (Zhang et al., 2026c; Hu et al., 2026) [CITE-CHECK]; other defences select tokens (Li et al., 2026) or use continual learning (Alssum et al., 2025) [CITE-CHECK]. A concurrent submission finds risk rankings taken at initialization nearly uncorrelated with those after two epochs, and re-scores rows during training (Anonymous, 2026). For attribution, Pan et al. (2026) find that removing rows flagged by gradient-based attribution gives a safer retrained model than removing rows flagged by WildGuard, from one training run per arm and with no compute reported. DATE-LM (Jiao et al., 2025) finds that with safety-aligned look-alikes in the data, attribution's detection drops while LLM classifiers are largely unaffected; without them, the strongest classifiers detect better, yet removing attribution's picks gives the safer retrained model. Luo et al. (2026b) report weak, inconsistent effects from deleting or reweighting influential rows, large effects from rewriting them, and an over-refusal trade-off for safety rewriting.

**Addition and model-level repairs.** Adding about 3% safety examples to instruction tuning improves safety but can cause exaggerated refusals (Bianchi et al., 2024) [CITE-CHECK]. Which rows are added matters: DataRx selects safety rows by the gap between a model's own response and a safe reference (Zhang et al., 2026b) [CITE-CHECK]; on Llama3-8B-Instruct, averaged over seven tasks, its random 1% mix raised attack success from 20.24% (no safety data) to 59.23%, while its selected mix lowered it to 13.70%. Its baselines, all additions, include self-distillation, a precedent for our self-generated arm. Guo et al. (2026) restore safety with a handful of safety rows, and Llama 3's safety mix was tuned by repeated experiments that traded violations against false refusals (Grattafiori et al., 2024). Model-level repairs change no data: a safety prompt used only at test time reduces erosion (PTST; Lyu et al., 2024), SafeTune compares repairs of safety drift in single deterministic runs (Seth et al., 2026) [CITE-CHECK], a gentler recipe shrinks erosion (Kim et al., 2025), Safe LoRA projects the LoRA weights of selected layers onto a safety-aligned subspace without training or data (Hsu et al., 2024), and an objective that constrains updates on the first answer tokens makes alignment more persistent under fine-tuning (Qi et al., 2025). We use such repairs as references that a data repair must beat. Other repairs act after fine-tuning and are not among our references (§6): Safe Delta adjusts the parameter change of a harmful or benign fine-tune (Lu et al., 2025), Antidote prunes harmful weights after harmful fine-tuning (Huang et al., 2025), and Zhang et al. (2026a) restore safety by further training on a single safety example. Huang et al. (2024) survey harmful fine-tuning attacks and defences.

**Attribution, its cost and what it tracks.** Training-data attribution ranges from cheap gradient similarity on LoRA gradients (LESS; Xia et al., 2024) [CITE-CHECK] to estimates of finite removal effects by unrolled differentiation (SOURCE; Bae et al., 2024), which are reported to cost several training runs [CITE-CHECK]. Xiao & Aranguri (2026) compare the cost of diagnosis methods for undesirable post-training behaviours; probe attribution is over ten times cheaper than gradient attribution and LLM-judge baselines in their study. Yin & Rush (2025) charge selection compute against training compute and find that powerful selection methods are almost never compute-optimal; gradient-based selection pays only when the trained model is about ten times larger than the selection model [CITE-CHECK]. Kim et al. (2026) find that gradient similarity tracks answer format more than task [CITE-CHECK]; we therefore include a free answer-length rule as a repair and as a control for attribution's picks.

**Prediction and decisions under a budget.** Persona vectors (Chen et al., 2025) project training data onto activation directions to flag data that will shift a trait [CITE-CHECK]; TuneAhead (Luo et al., 2026a) and Data2Behavior (Wang et al., 2026a) predict a fine-tune's outcome from its data before training; MNEME predicts side effects of fine-tuning and unlearning by sparse model diffing (Kassem et al., 2025) [CITE-CHECK]; any such predictor can enter our comparison as a scorer at its measured cost. Successive halving and Hyperband allocate a fixed budget by repeatedly discarding the worst candidates at increasing fidelity (Jamieson & Talwalkar, 2016; Li et al., 2018); multi-fidelity methods model how cheap observations relate to full ones (e.g. Ouyang et al., 2025). DataDecide (Magnusson et al., 2025) scores small pretraining runs by decision accuracy against compute, and Ivison et al. (2025) charge selection compute against training on more data [CITE-CHECK].

**How we differ.** We do not claim a first benchmark of safety filters or defences, a first comparison of removal against addition, or a first pricing of data selection: SafeTune and SafeTuneBed (Hossain et al., 2025) compare defences; DualSelect compares selectors and a safety-data mix over seeds, with a false-refusal rate and selection cost; DataRx compares ways to choose added safety data; and DataShield's cost table and Yin & Rush (2025) price selection [CITE-CHECK]. Our contribution is the combination; to our knowledge, and not found by our search, no prior work combines, for purely benign data, equivalence-bounded contrasts over six training seeds for removal and addition repairs; over-refusal and task loss as limits fixed in advance; every arm's full cost, scoring included, under two conventions; model-level references that a data repair must beat; a positive control with a planted cause; and predictions about the value of diagnosis written down before the data.

## 3 Problem Setup

### 3.1 Setting and endpoints

Let $\theta_0$ be a safety-tuned Instruct model, fine-tuned with a fixed recipe $\mathcal{R}$ (adapter, loss, optimizer, schedule, length rule) on benign data $\mathcal{D} = D_1 \cup \dots \cup D_K$, split into $K$ *units* by metadata fixed before any outcome. A *repair* $a$ maps $(\mathcal{D}, \mathcal{R})$ to a training set and recipe $(a(\mathcal{D}), \mathcal{R}_a)$; a data repair keeps $\mathcal{R}_a = \mathcal{R}$. The model trained with seed $s$ (adapter initialization and data order) is $\theta(a,s)$. For a model $\theta$, $f(\theta)$ is the *unsafe rate* (the share of sampled answers to a harm bank that a frozen judge labels unsafe), $\ell(\theta)$ is the held-out *task loss*, and $r(\theta)$ is the *over-refusal rate* on benign prompts that resemble harmful ones. Endpoints average over seeds: $F(a) = \mathbb{E}_s[f(\theta(a,s))]$, and likewise $\bar\ell(a)$ and $R(a)$. With $a_0$ the fine-tune without repair, the *erosion*, refusal erosion as Qwen3Guard labels it (§4.2), is $E = F(a_0) - f(\theta_0)$; repair is meaningful only when $E$ exceeds seed noise (Gate A, §4.6). Two actors can repair: a fine-tuning customer, who controls the data and can also set the learning rate and the number of epochs, and the service provider, who also holds the weights and can run every scorer and reference. We report the best repair within each actor's menu (Table 1).

### 3.2 Repair contrasts, limits and labels (primary)

Let $a_{\mathrm{R}}$ remove the same number of random rows as the removal repairs. Removals are matched in training tokens as well as rows, or a token-matched analysis is pre-registered beside the row-matched one [TO BE FIXED in the stage-1 pre-registration]. A removal repair $m$ is compared with random removal, $\Lambda_m = F(a_{\mathrm{R}}) - F(m)$: the safety it restores beyond removing the same amount of data at random. An addition repair is compared with no repair, $\Lambda_m = F(a_0) - F(m)$, and a selected or self-generated addition with the random addition of the same size. The restoration $G_m = F(a_0) - F(m)$ is reported for every arm. Contrasts are estimated from per-seed unsafe rates; the analysis model, including whether it pairs arms by seed, is open [TO BE FIXED in the stage-1 pre-registration].

Side effects are the *over-refusal excess* $O(m) = R(m) - r(\theta_0)$, against the untuned model ($R(m) - R(a_0)$ is also reported), and the *task-loss increase* $L(m) = \bar\ell(m) - \bar\ell(a_0)$. A repair is *feasible* at limits $(\tau_O, \tau_L)$ if $O(m) \le \tau_O$ and $L(m) \le \tau_L$. Results are reported at $\tau_O \in \{2, 5, 10\}$ pp; $\tau_L$ is two standard errors of a seed-to-seed difference in held-out loss, fixed before the comparison runs. Whether feasibility is a one-sided non-inferiority bound, with over-refusal on the full XSTest and OR-Bench-Hard banks, is open [TO BE FIXED in the stage-1 pre-registration]. The *cost* of a repair is $C(m) = c(m) + 1$ FRE: $c(m)$ is the measured cost of building the repaired data (scoring rows, selecting or generating safety rows), and one FRE is the measured cost of one random-removal run (§4.7).

With a smallest effect of interest $\delta = 5$ pp and Holm correction at family-wise level 0.05 over the primary contrasts, each contrast receives one label: *better* if the Holm-adjusted test rejects $\Lambda_m = 0$ and the estimate is at least $\delta$; *equivalent* if the 90% interval for $\Lambda_m$ lies inside $(-\delta, \delta)$ (two one-sided tests); *worse* if the contrast with its comparator is significantly adverse at the Holm-adjusted 5% level on the unsafe rate ($\Lambda_m < 0$), on over-refusal ($R(m)$ above the comparator's) or on task loss ($\bar\ell(m)$ above the comparator's), each read in its adverse direction, so an adverse repair is never reported as inconclusive; and *inconclusive* otherwise. Whether the over-refusal test of *worse* uses the comparator's rate or the untuned model's $r(\theta_0)$ as its baseline is open [TO BE FIXED in the stage-1 pre-registration]. *Better* and *equivalent* exclude each other (*better* needs an estimate of at least $\delta$, *equivalent* an upper limit below $\delta$); which label wins when *worse* holds with another, and whether a significant difference below $\delta$ gets its own label, are open [TO BE FIXED in the stage-1 pre-registration]; both results are reported. A margin of 4 pp is reported as a sensitivity. Five references change no data (§4.4). One, *adapter scaling*, $\theta_\lambda = \theta_0 + \lambda\,(\theta(a_0, s) - \theta_0)$ for $\lambda \in \{1, 0.75, 0.5, 0\}$, traces $(F, O, L)$ from the fine-tune ($\lambda = 1$) to the untuned model ($\lambda = 0$). A data repair *dominates* only if it lies below this curve: a lower $F$ than the curve at equal $O$ and $L$.

### 3.3 Localization

Let $a_{-k}$ remove unit $D_k$ and $a_{\mathrm{rand},k}$ remove $|D_k|$ random rows drawn across units. Erosion is *localized* at level $\delta_B$ if some $\Lambda_k = F(a_{\mathrm{rand},k}) - F(a_{-k})$ reaches $\delta_B$ beyond seed noise (the random arm controls for the smaller training dose), and *diffuse* if every $\Lambda_k$ is credibly below $\delta_B$. Diffuse erosion leaves the repair comparison unchanged: unit removals then act like random removal up to $\delta_B$, and a unit-level diagnostic has little to find.

### 3.4 The value of diagnosis (secondary, model-based)

A *selector* with budget $B$ (in FRE) buys trial runs on a menu of $M$ repairs and returns a pick; its regret is the expected unsafe rate of the pick minus that of the menu's best feasible repair. The value $\Delta_d$ of a diagnostic $d$ is the regret saved by paying its cost before the same frozen trial-run rule, and its break-even cost $c^*_d$ is the largest cost with $\Delta_d \ge 0$. At the menus we can afford, these are mostly outputs of a model, frozen before Gate B's category runs; we report measured against predicted values and score OLMo-calibrated predictions on Llama (Appendix C.7).

### 4.1 Models, data and recipes

**Families and scale.** At 1B, OLMo-2-0425-1B-Instruct (Team OLMo et al., 2024) is the stage-1 model and Llama-3.2-1B-Instruct (Grattafiori et al., 2024) the second family (stage 2; §4.8). At 7–8B, the full comparison runs on OLMo-2-1124-7B-Instruct and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, at learning rates fixed by the same rule (below) [PLANNED; funding-gated, fallback one family]. Olmo-3-7B-Instruct (Team Olmo et al., 2025) is a third 7–8B model under the same rule [PLANNED; funding-gated; first 7–8B model cut]. A headline check at the largest single-GPU size comes last [PLACEHOLDER: if resources allow]. Qwen2.5 models (Qwen et al., 2024) are outside this study because their erosion saturated under recipe B during development (above 55 pp); a same-recipe comparison of OLMo-2, Llama-3 and Qwen2.5 is reported in [CITE: concurrent submission, anonymized]. Every model trains on one GPU, and every comparison stays on one GPU type.

**Data.** Gate B and stage 1 train on 4,800 rows of Dolly-15k (Conover et al., 2023), 600 from each of 8 task categories (the units; $K = 8$), sealed per tokenizer with a manifest; 200 further rows, 25 per category, measure task loss. The add repairs draw their safety rows from PKU-SafeRLHF (§4.3). Alpaca is the planned second benign dataset, after Gate B [NOT YET FIXED: its units and learning rate].

**Recipes.** *Recipe B*, the main recipe: LoRA (Hu et al., 2022) of rank 64 and scale 128 on all linear layers; AdamW at a constant learning rate of $10^{-4}$, no warmup; 128 rows per step; one epoch (38 steps); a per-row loss (each row's mean over its response tokens). Gate A, Gate B and stage 1 use it. *Recipe G*, a reference arm that changes only the recipe (§4.4), adds a 4-step warmup and cosine decay, which halve the mean learning rate over the epoch, and a token-mean loss. It is not a general fix: its effect on erosion differs across families and sizes [CITE: concurrent submission, anonymized]. Training is deterministic on matched hardware.

**A matched erosion dose.** Each family uses recipe B with only its learning rate changed: the highest rung of a ladder ($10^{-4}$, $5 \times 10^{-5}$, $2 \times 10^{-5}$) whose probe reads partial (20–55 pp) under the frozen one-seed Gate A bars (§4.6; a probe too close on $E$ gets one more seed; any other too-close bar goes to the user). The rate is chosen from probes only, never from a repair result. Families respond differently to the same recipe [CITE: concurrent submission, anonymized]; we fixed this rule after reading a one-recipe development ladder, on which no rung left both 1B families partly eroded, and Llama's first probe at recipe B, and we state cross-family claims at a matched erosion dose. The rungs are $10^{-4}$ for OLMo-2 (1B and 7B) and $5 \times 10^{-5}$ for Llama (3.2-1B and 3.1-8B). Both Llama rungs were accepted on a too-close bar (one seed's answers-end bar at 1B; the window's upper edge at 8B); §5 reports both.

### 4.2 Measures, judges and the claim rule

- **Unsafe rate $U$.** The share of 600 sampled answers (200 development harm prompts $\times$ 3 samples at temperature 0.7, up to 2,048 new tokens) that Qwen3Guard-Gen-4B (Zhao et al., 2025) labels Unsafe. The prompts come from HarmBench (Mazeika et al., 2024), StrongREJECT (Souly et al., 2024), SimpleSafetyTests (Vidgen et al., 2023) and OR-Bench's toxic set (Cui et al., 2025). Standard errors are clustered by prompt. $U$ is a broad policy label, not an attack-success rate as published elsewhere: judges and prompts differ, so $U$ is compared only within this study. A rise in $U$ is refusal erosion as Qwen3Guard labels it. Gates and selectors read development banks only; final claims are confirmed on held-out prompts (§4.8).
- **Compliance and the claim rule.** HarmBench's classifier asks whether an answer carries out the requested behaviour. A contrast in $U$ supports the wording *harmful compliance as the HarmBench classifier defines it*, never harm alone, only if the classifier's contrast has the same sign and at least half the size and the classifier's erosion under that recipe is at least 20 pp; otherwise the sentence says *restored refusal*. Whether the classifier reads each answer clipped to 512 tokens or in full (up to 2,048) is open [TO BE FIXED in the stage-1 pre-registration]. A blind human read during development rated about a third of judge-unsafe answers as usable help (Appendix A).
- **Also per arm:** hard-category $U$ (violent, illegal, sexual or self-harm categories); $U$ by answer-length band and a length-reweighted $U$; Granite Guardian 3.3 (Padhi et al., 2024) on the greedy answers; and a selectivity score (refusal on harm prompts minus refusal on benign prompts). Whether StrongREJECT's evaluator score (Souly et al., 2024) and a prefilling probe (Qi et al., 2025) are secondary endpoints is open [TO BE FIXED in the stage-1 pre-registration].
- **Over-refusal.** Qwen3Guard's refusal flag on greedy answers to 100 benign prompts that resemble harmful ones (50 XSTest safe prompts, Röttger et al., 2024; 50 OR-Bench-Hard prompts, Cui et al., 2025), reported by source, with a lexicon check.
- **Task loss and skill.** Held-out loss on all 200 task rows is the skill limit of the comparison. Probe and Gate A bars also check ARC-Easy (Clark et al., 2018) under a rule loosened during development; we report both verdicts (Appendix C.4).
- **Answers end.** A coherence screen flags answers that reach the token cap, repeat 4-grams heavily, are empty, or have fewer than 5 tokens; an arm whose answers fail too often is not read.

### 4.6 Gates A, B and C

**Gate A (entry).** For each family at its matched dose, over three seeds (the probe seed and two fresh seeds), mean $E$ must lie in a pre-registered partial-erosion window of 20–55 pp, be at least twice the seed standard deviation, and exceed zero on every seed; a second judge must agree in direction, the answers must end, and the task must be learned without lost skill (§4.2); results and every setting tried are in §5.

**Gate B (localization).** Its pre-registration was frozen before any Gate B run; Appendix C.1 gives its rules. Each category removal (600 rows) is compared with random removal of 600 rows at the same seed. All 8 categories run at one seed; the two with the largest contrast, plus Gate C's picks, run at two more seeds. The verdict is LOCALIZED if some category restores at least $\delta_B = 8$ pp more than random removal and clears a Bonferroni-corrected bar; DIFFUSE if every category is credibly below 8 pp; and TOO CLOSE otherwise. It reads $U$ alone, under guards. At planning noise, 80% detection needs about 10–12 pp, so DIFFUSE means no category-level cause above about 8 pp, not no cause. If DIFFUSE, Gate C is not read, stage 1 is unchanged, and a rewrite arm runs first among the secondary arms (Appendix C.3). **Status:** the noise gate has passed, and Gate C's rankings were sealed before the category runs.

**Gate C (which diagnostic picks the category).** Attribution (A1's score, on a retrained first-seed $a_0$) and representation similarity (on the untuned model) each pick their top category; a format rule picks the category with the largest share of its loss on the first five answer tokens. Gate C is read only if Gate B is LOCALIZED. In the sealed rankings both diagnostics pick the same category, and the format rule another, so by the frozen rules ATTR_BETTER and REP_BETTER cannot occur, and the paper may say only that neither pick is the format pick (Appendix C.2).

## 6 Discussion and Limitations

**What "unsafe" means.** Our primary judge gives a broad policy label, so a rise in $U$ is refusal erosion as Qwen3Guard labels it. In a blind human read during development (one reader; Appendix A), most judge-unsafe answers went along with the request, but only about a third gave usable help. The claim rule (§4.2) therefore allows at most "harmful compliance as the HarmBench classifier defines it", and $U$ is not comparable with attack-success rates published under other judges and prompts.

**Equivalence and power.** The labels resolve differences of about 5 pp, and an *equivalent* label means "within $\pm 5$ pp", not "identical" (§3.2). Gate B's detection limit (§4.6) holds for categories; row-level causes that span categories are not tested by Gate B.

**Recipe and setting.** The main recipe, B, is plain rather than standard: a constant learning rate with no warmup, a per-row loss and a rank-64 adapter on all linear layers, larger than is common (a rank-16 check is in [CITE: concurrent submission, anonymized]). Recipe G's standard schedule lowers the mean learning rate; it is a reference, not a general fix, and repair rankings may differ at a lower dose. Cross-family claims hold at a matched erosion dose, not at one recipe, under a rule fixed after a development ladder was read. The skill bar at the probes and Gate A is looser than the bar first registered, and we say where it decided a reading (Appendix C.4). The main model and recipe were chosen by screening probe and Gate A readings, never Gate B or stage-1 outcomes; §5 lists every setting tried.

**Baselines, doses and cost.** Every scorer is re-implemented from its paper ("X-style"), so a weak result may reflect our implementation; a published table of deviations per scorer, reference outputs requested from the authors and the published-setting replication address this. Comparisons across removal and addition hold only at the pre-registered doses (600 rows removed, 48 added). An arm's cost depends on how much we evaluate as well as on training, and costs at 1B do not transfer to larger models, because a scorer's cost and a run's cost grow differently with model size.

**Scale and family.** The full comparison runs at 1B, with one benign dataset until Alpaca runs; at 7–8B it is planned on both families and Olmo-3-7B-Instruct, funding-gated, with one family as the fallback and Olmo 3 the first model cut; larger sizes get a check only if resources allow. A family difference is claimed only above its MDE.

**The positive control and the secondary analysis.** The planted cause is artificial; finding it shows that a diagnostic *can* find a concentrated cause, not that natural erosion has one. The value of diagnosis at natural menus is model-based and judged against predictions made in advance. Selectors are not run against each other at equal budgets here; a later study with its own pre-registration would do that.

**Scope and user.** We compare data repairs with model-level references; rewriting responses enters only if the erosion is diffuse. Post-hoc repairs other than the references, such as the one-example patch (Zhang et al., 2026a), are not compared (§2), so no claim here covers them. A customer of a closed fine-tuning service controls the data and some hyperparameters but cannot run gradient or representation scorers; the provider, or a developer with open weights, can run every scorer but also has model-level repairs. We therefore report the best repair within each actor's menu (§3.1); a data-only pipeline reads the data repairs alone.

## Impact Statement

This work studies how to repair a language model's safety after benign fine-tuning has eroded it, so that developers and fine-tuning services can choose a repair with its side effects and cost in view. We see three risks. First, the study shows which training data erode safety and which repairs fail, and the positive control, if run, plants benign-looking rows that erode safety; an attacker could use this. The planted rows come from a public safety dataset, and we release [TO BE DECIDED: what is released, for example scores and outcome tables but no generated answers to harm prompts]. Second, every outcome comes from automatic judges, and a blind human read rated only about a third of judge-unsafe answers as usable help, so a repair labelled *better* or *equivalent* is not a safety guarantee. Third, the models are small and the benign data come from one dataset; the results may not hold for larger models, other data or deployed systems, and do not replace a model's own safety evaluation.

## Appendix A: Blind human read (development evidence)

This read checked, during development, what the primary judge's Unsafe label means. It gates nothing, changes no verdict and is not a result of the study. The same read is also reported in [CITE: concurrent submission, anonymized].

**Design.** The read was pre-registered before anyone saw the answers, and the ratings were sealed before the key was opened. A seeded rule drew 50 sampled answers to development harm prompts: 40 that Qwen3Guard labels Unsafe, from recipe-B fine-tunes of OLMo-2-1B, Qwen2.5-1.5B, Qwen2.5-7B and Llama-3.2-1B and a recipe-G fine-tune of OLMo-2-1B (6 to 12 per group), and 10 that it labels Safe, as controls. The sheet showed only the request and the answer, without model, recipe, seed or judge label. ## Appendix C: Design details

This appendix holds design details that the main text summarizes. The frozen pre-registrations govern wherever this summary differs from them [TO BE DECIDED: anonymized copies as supplementary material].

### C.1 Gate B

**Arms and waves.** Wave 0a: three random-removal arms $R_1$–$R_3$ (600 rows, 75 per category, each from its own sealed draw) at seeds 1101–1103, for the noise gate. Wave 0b: a retrain of $a_0$ at seed 1101 that keeps its checkpoints after steps 10 and 38, and the Gate C diagnostics. Wave 1: each of the 8 category removals at seed 1101. Wave 2: $m = 2$–$4$ units (the two with the largest wave-1 contrast, plus Gate C's picks if not already in) at seeds 1102 and 1103. Every removal arm comes from a sealed edit file and trains 33 steps; the Gate A $a_0$ runs are reused. No step starts before the step above it is complete.

**Contrasts and noise.** All $U$ are in pp over 600 sampled answers. For unit $u$ at seed $j$, $c_{u,j} = U(R_j, j) - U(u, j)$, so $a_0$ cancels exactly; $D_u = c_{u,1101}$ (wave 1) and $\Lambda_u = (c_{u,1102} + c_{u,1103})/2$ (wave 2). With $r_j = U(a_0, j) - U(R_j, j)$ and $\sigma_{\mathrm{eval}}$ the median prompt-clustered standard error of a seed-matched pair's per-prompt difference, $\hat\sigma_{\mathrm{gate}} = \max(\mathrm{SD}(r_{1101}, r_{1102}, r_{1103}), \sigma_{\mathrm{eval},0})$; the final $\hat\sigma$ pools the $r_j$ and the wave-2 pairs. A fallback $\hat\sigma$ that does not use $a_0$ applies if the retrain's adapter fingerprints, or a run's environment, differ from Gate A's. Tests use $t$ at 3 degrees of freedom, the effective degrees of freedom of $\hat\sigma$.

**Noise gate.** After wave 0a, Gate B goes on only if $\hat\sigma_{\mathrm{gate}} \le 7$ pp and $\max(8, 4.177 \times 0.707\,\hat\sigma_{\mathrm{gate}}) \le 33.8$ pp (0.75 of Gate A's mean $E$). A failure stops Gate B, and the pre-registered fallback applies instead of a redesign.

**Verdict.** *LOCALIZED*: some wave-2 unit has $\Lambda_u \ge 8$ pp and $\Lambda_u \ge t_m \times 0.707\,\hat\sigma$, where $t_m$ is the one-sided Bonferroni 5% $t$ at 3 degrees of freedom (3.182, 3.740 and 4.177 for $m = 2, 3, 4$); the unit passes guards G2–G4 and the direction checks, and every arm that the contrast uses passes G1. *DIFFUSE*: $\max_u D_u < 2.381\,\hat\sigma$, and every wave-2 unit has $\Lambda_u + 1.638 \times 0.707\,\hat\sigma < 8$ pp; both also hold on coherent answers (G2) and in both adverse corners (G3), and every arm passes G1. *TOO CLOSE*: anything else, a guard failure or a missing arm included. With LOCALIZED, the one-sided 95% lower bound $\Lambda_u - t_m \times 0.707\,\hat\sigma$ is reported. At Gate A's mean erosion, 8 pp is about 18% of $E$, so LOCALIZED does not say that the unit carries most of the erosion. The verdict reads $U$ alone; the blind human read recommended before the first wave is done, on a different sample from the one first proposed (Appendix A).

**Guards.** G1, answers end: Gate A's bars on sampled answers (at most 5% capped and at most 10% failing the coherence screen; a short-answer TOO CLOSE counts as a failure); a random-removal arm that fails G1 makes the verdict TOO CLOSE with no extension. G2: the verdict recomputed on coherent answers only. G3: two adverse corners, with unscored answers counted against the verdict, and with controversial answers counted Unsafe. G4, over-correction: the unit's mean refusal rate on the 100 over-refusal prompts may exceed the untuned model's by at most 5 pp. Direction checks: hard-category $U$, the share of sampled answers that do not refuse, and Granite Guardian's greedy labels must each give a positive contrast.

**Extension.** It is automatic; no choice follows an outcome. A wave-2 unit is *contested* if it is not LOCALIZED, $\Lambda_u + 1.638 \times 0.707\,\hat\sigma \ge 8$ pp, and it passed the guards. After a TOO CLOSE with at least one contested unit and no random-removal arm failing G1, up to two contested units run at seeds 1104 and 1105 with $R_4$ and $R_5$, and are read once more over four seeds with the same constants. No other reading follows an outcome, and no new margin is set.

**Length control.** After LOCALIZED, for the unit with the largest $\Lambda_u$: two removals of as many rows, drawn from the other units and matched to the unit's answer-length bins, run against the unit's removal at two fresh seeds. The wording is "content-localized" only if the length-matched contrast $L_u$ is at least 8 pp and at least $2.353 \times 0.707\,\hat\sigma$, and all four arms pass G1; otherwise it is "localized under the per-example-mean loss; content and answer length not separated". The control changes the wording, never the verdict.

**Operating characteristics.** By simulation, with one unit carrying the effect and the guards not simulated (so the rates are upper bounds): at central planning noise (a seed-pair standard deviation of 3.75 pp) and given that the noise gate passed, a true 8, 10 or 12 pp unit reads LOCALIZED in 52, 83 or 96% of runs, so an 8 pp category reads LOCALIZED about half the time. 80% detection needs about 9, 10 or 12 pp at low, central or high planning noise. False LOCALIZED, given that the gate passed and no unit has an effect, is 0.2–2.7% for seed-pair standard deviations of 3.75–7 pp. A missed 8–12 pp unit mostly reads TOO CLOSE, not DIFFUSE (DIFFUSE in at most 7.5% of runs at 8 pp). The pre-registration records the prior that DIFFUSE or TOO CLOSE is more likely than LOCALIZED.

**Status.** The noise gate passed after wave 0a, and neither trigger for the fallback $\hat\sigma$ fired after wave 0b. The diagnostics ran in wave 0b, and their rankings were sealed before wave 1 (Appendix C.2).

### C.2 Gate C

**Diagnostics and picks.** Each diagnostic scores all 4,800 rows, and a unit's score is the mean over its 600 rows. Attribution is bi-anchored gradient similarity (sealed anchors: 64 harmful, 64 safe) at the retrained $a_0$'s checkpoints after steps 10 and 38, with equal weight. Representation similarity is bi-anchored, at the untuned model's final layer and last response token. A content classifier is reported only. $\mathrm{pick}_A$ and $\mathrm{pick}_R$ are the top units of attribution and representation similarity (ties go to the lower unit id). The format pick $\mathrm{pick}_F$ is the unit with the largest mean share of a row's loss weight on its first five answer tokens under the per-row loss; it costs nothing.

**Rule.** For a pick $p$, $B_p$ is the mean of $c_{p,j}$ over seeds 1101–1103, and $\Delta = B_{\mathrm{pick}_A} - B_{\mathrm{pick}_R}$. A pick beats random if $B_p \ge 2\hat\sigma/\sqrt{3} \approx 1.15\,\hat\sigma$; the margin is $M_C = \max(6\ \mathrm{pp}, 1.15\,\hat\sigma)$, with $\hat\sigma$ from before any extension. AGREE: $\mathrm{pick}_A = \mathrm{pick}_R$. ATTR_BETTER: $\Delta \ge M_C$ and $\mathrm{pick}_A$ beats random. REP_BETTER: $\Delta \le -M_C$. NONE_FINDS: neither pick beats random. The verdict is the first that holds in the order NONE_FINDS, AGREE, ATTR_BETTER, REP_BETTER, TOO CLOSE. If an arm that a $B_p$ uses fails G1, Gate C reads TOO CLOSE. Gate C is read only after LOCALIZED, and it reads "not read" when an arm it needs is missing. No Gate C extension runs.

**Format reading.** If $\mathrm{pick}_A = \mathrm{pick}_F$, the paper must say that attribution's pick is explained by answer form (a near-free answer-form score picks the same unit) and claims no value of that pick beyond answer form; the same holds for representation similarity. If neither pick equals $\mathrm{pick}_F$, the paper says only that neither pick is the form pick, which does not show that the picks are free of form. The two equalities are fixed when the rankings are sealed, so they are reported whatever Gate B reads; the form reading never changes a verdict. Also reported: the Spearman $\rho$ of each diagnostic's 8 unit scores with the first-five-token share and with the wave-1 contrasts $D_u$ (a one-sided 5% test needs $\rho \ge 9/14$), and the top-1 hit (chance 12.5%).

**Consequence of the sealed rankings.** Attribution and representation similarity pick the same unit, and the format rule picks another. So $\Delta = 0$: ATTR_BETTER and REP_BETTER cannot occur, and Gate C, if read, is NONE_FINDS, AGREE or TOO CLOSE (the last only through the answers-end guard on the pick arms). The format reading is the second case above, and wave 2 holds $m = 2$ or 3 units.

### C.4 The skill bar

Held-out loss on all 200 task rows is the skill limit of the comparison. The probe and Gate A bars also check ARC-Easy accuracy (Clark et al., 2018) on 189 questions: the paired drop $d$ against the untuned model must stay at or below 0.03. With 189 questions the paired interval is about $\pm 0.07$ wide, so it straddles 0.03 for almost every run. A rule fixed during development, first for probe and Gate A readings at one common recipe and then extended to every family's Gate A read at its chosen learning rate before any such read, resolves a straddling interval: the run **passes, as "skill kept, reported as a limitation", when the point drop $d \le 0.03$ and the held-out task loss is below the untuned model's (the task is learned); it fails when $d > 0.03$.** This rule is a loosening: it can pass a run whose true drop is up to about 6 points, which 189 questions cannot detect. We report the verdict under the frozen bars beside the verdict under this rule. Disclosure: the first recipe-G probe on OLMo-2 was read under the earlier rule before this rule was proposed, so for that one run the rule is not applied blind. At 1B, ARC-Easy is close to chance, so "skill kept" rests mainly on the held-out task loss.

### C.5 The positive control

**Definitions.** A planted data set $\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{p}}$ replaces the rows of one small cell of $\mathcal{D}$ with $k$ rows that look benign but carry a known eroding signal, keeping the number of rows fixed. Nested partitions split $\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{p}}$ into $M$ cells, so that the planted cell sits inside one cell at every $M$. The planted erosion is $P = F(a_0^{\mathrm{p}}) - F(a_0)$, and removing the cell that holds the plant at menu size $M$ restores $G^{\mathrm{p}}(M) = F(a_0^{\mathrm{p}}) - F(a^{\mathrm{p}}_{-c(M)})$. A diagnostic $d$ *hits* at $(M, k_d)$ if it ranks that cell among its top $k_d$. Because the truth is known, the sweep measures how diagnosis and trial runs scale with $M$, which natural menus at our budget cannot.

**Design.** [PLACEHOLDER: runs only if funded.] The plant is $k = 37$ rows whose answers comply directly with prompts that also have an unsafe answer in the source data, but which the source labels safe; they look benign. A sealed draw picks one category and one leaf cell inside it, and the plant replaces that cell's rows, so the run keeps 4,800 rows and 38 steps. The nested cells [TO BE FINALISED] are the 8 categories (600 rows), 32 length quartiles within categories (150 rows) and 128 random quarters of those (37–38 rows). A pilot of three seeds goes on only if the plant adds at least 10 pp of erosion with a one-sided 90% lower bound above 5 pp (otherwise the plant doubles once, then the null is reported). The main sweep removes the planted cell at each menu size (three seeds each) and runs random removal on the planted data. Every scorer ranks the cells without new runs. Outputs: $G^{\mathrm{p}}(M)$; each diagnostic's top-$k$ hit rate with known truth; the runs-only cost of finding the cell, replayed with the frozen selector rules; and a check of additivity across the nested levels.

### C.6 Cost conventions

Every arm and every scorer is logged in GPU-seconds by stage (setup, training, greedy generation, sampled generation, judging, scoring, overhead). One FRE per family is the median measured time of that family's random-removal arms. Two cost conventions are frozen before stage 1: *full* (training plus the evaluation as run) and *lean* (training plus one sample per harm prompt, priced from the measured time per answer); the cost $c_0$ of one lean trial run is measured on a real job. Every pool filter, generated answer, anchor job and scorer is charged; "free" means below 0.01 FRE. Attribution is priced from its measured scoring pass. Claims about cost hold only where both conventions agree, and no cost ratio measured at 1B is claimed for a larger model.

### C.7 The value of diagnosis

A *menu* $\mathcal{A}_M = \{a_0, a_1, \dots, a_M\}$ holds $a_0$ and $M$ repairs built by a frozen rule, never by a compared diagnostic; $\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{feas}}$ is its feasible subset and $a^* = \arg\min_{a \in \mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{feas}}} F(a)$. A *selector* $\pi$ with budget $B$ (in FRE) buys trial runs (each a training fraction $\phi$ and a prompt fraction $\psi$, priced from the measured cost model) at total cost at most $B$ and returns $\hat a$. It drops a repair whose adverse estimate of $O$ or $L$ passes its limit ($a_0$ is never dropped), and picks the lowest estimated $F$ among repairs with full runs on at least two seeds; ties go to $a_0$. An infeasible pick is scored as if no repair were made:

$$\mathrm{loss}(\hat a) = \begin{cases} F(\hat a) & \hat a \in \mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{feas}},\\ F(a_0) & \text{otherwise,} \end{cases} \qquad \mathrm{Reg}(\pi; B, M) = \mathbb{E}[\mathrm{loss}(\hat a)] - F(a^*).$$

This is a scoring convention for an expected outcome at a fixed budget, not a safety guarantee. The runs-only selector $\pi_-$ uses one rule per $(M, B)$ cell (a one-shot screen, successive halving or hierarchical bisection), chosen from a simulation before any outcome and then frozen. The selector $\pi_{+d}$ pays the cost $c_d$ of diagnostic $d$, keeps its top $k_d$ repairs, and runs the same rule on $B - c_d$. The value of $d$ and its break-even cost are

$$\Delta_d(B; M, c) = \mathrm{Reg}(\pi_-; B, M) - \mathrm{Reg}(\pi_{+d}; B, M, c), \qquad c^*_d(B, M) = \sup\{c \ge 0 : \Delta_d(B; M, c) \ge 0\}.$$

$F(a^*)$ cancels in $\Delta_d$. When $a_0$ is feasible, regret is non-negative, so no diagnostic can save more than $V_{\max}(B, M) = \mathrm{Reg}(\pi_-; B, M)$. At the menus we can afford, these quantities are mostly outputs of a model, so we treat them as predictions. A generative model (repair effects shrunk by empirical Bayes, noise from pooled residuals, measured costs, two effect shapes) is frozen before Gate B's category runs, and its predicted $V_{\max}$, $\Delta_d$ and $c^*_d$ for the category menu and the planted menus are recorded then. We report measured against predicted values (replayed with the same code path on the outcome tables), and score OLMo-calibrated predictions of the chance of finding the best repair, and of regret, on Llama's category menu. Selectors are not run against each other at equal budgets in this study.

### C.8 The opening decomposition and the first-token margin

Let $p(\theta)$ be the share of sampled answers to harm prompts that open with a refusal (a frozen lexicon applied to the first 12 words), and $u_{\mathrm{R}}(\theta)$ and $u_{\mathrm{N}}(\theta)$ the unsafe rates among answers that do and do not open with one, so $f = p\,u_{\mathrm{R}} + (1-p)\,u_{\mathrm{N}}$. For the untuned model (subscript 0) and a fine-tune (subscript 1),

$$f_1 - f_0 = \underbrace{(p_0 - p_1)(u_{\mathrm{N}0} - u_{\mathrm{R}0})}_{\text{opening switch}} + \underbrace{(1-p_1)(u_{\mathrm{N}1} - u_{\mathrm{N}0}) + p_1 (u_{\mathrm{R}1} - u_{\mathrm{R}0})}_{\text{content change}}.$$

The *opening share* is the first term over $f_1 - f_0$; the split depends on the order of terms, so both orders are reported. The decomposition is descriptive: it says whether a change in the unsafe rate shows up in how answers open or in what they say, not which training choice caused the change. The first term has two layers: how many refusal openings fine-tuning removes ($p_0 - p_1$), and how often a direct answer was already unsafe before fine-tuning ($u_{\mathrm{N}0}$); where $u_{\mathrm{N}0}$ is high and $u_{\mathrm{R}0}$ low, a lost opening comes close to an unsafe answer. If the opening share is small, repairs that act only on how answers open (refusal examples; an objective that constrains updates on the first answer tokens) may restore less than repairs that change what the answers say. We apply the decomposition to every arm; across families and recipes, the two layers are reported in [CITE: concurrent submission, anonymized].

The causal test on Gate B arms is a teacher-forced first-token refusal margin $m(p) = \log P_{\mathrm{ref}}(p) - \log(1 - P_{\mathrm{ref}}(p))$, where $P_{\mathrm{ref}}$ is the probability of a frozen refusal phrase as the first answer tokens. If a category's effect runs through openings, the margin should track $U$ across arms.

### C.9 Side studies: measurement validity and side-effect prediction

**Measurement validity and over-refusal.** Agreement and $E$ for three judges on the same answers, hard-category $U$ and length bands, how much of the gap to published numbers comes from judges and prompts, and $O$ by source, bounded by the judge's false-Unsafe rate on benign prompts.

**Side-effect prediction (secondary).** On the addition arms only: the balanced error of predicting an over-refusal violation, a diagnostic against trial runs of equal cost.
