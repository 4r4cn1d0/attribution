**Date.** Written 16 Oct 2026, 21:10–21:30 UTC (clock checked with `date -u`; this is 17 Oct in India). Read-only research. No runs, no commits.

**Paper.** "Repairing Safety After Benign Fine-Tuning: A Compute-Matched Comparison of Data Repairs" (`writing/paper/2026-10-08-icml-draft.md`, §1–§2 and the reference list read).

**Window.** Work posted or accepted 1 Aug – 16 Oct 2026, plus earlier work that the draft's reference list does not have.

---

## 0. Bottom line (read this first)

1. **No new paper does the whole study.** I found no paper that compares removal repairs *and* addition repairs for erosion by purely benign data, with each repair's full cost measured, over-refusal and task loss as limits, equivalence-bounded verdicts over several seeds, and model-level references. The combination is still open.
2. **But each part now has a precedent, and the draft understates two of them.**
   - **DualSelect is closer than the draft says.** The draft says it covers "not addition repairs, model-level references or measured cost". In fact it compares six selectors *and* one safety-data mix (SafeInstr) over three seeds, reports a false-refusal rate, and reports selection time (1.34× standard fine-tuning) at a matched data budget. Its data include red-teaming pairs, so it is not purely benign. It has no model-level references. **This sentence in §1 must change** (exact text in §5).
   - **DataRx already compares six ways of choosing added safety data**, one of them self-distillation (close to our "self-generated refusals" arm). It reports no seeds and no cost.
   - **SABA (ICLR 2027 submission) beats nineteen baselines "under the same training budget"** on benign fine-tuning, and shows that risk scores taken once at the start of training are nearly uncorrelated with scores after two epochs. Our attribution and LARF-style arms score once, at the start.
3. **"Compute-matched" is the weakest word in the title.** The draft's design is *cost-accounted* (every arm logged in GPU-seconds and FRE, §4.7). The removals are *row-matched* (600 rows), not compute- or token-matched. A new submission ("Token Mass, Not Geometry") shows that a selection gain at a fixed document budget vanished once token budgets were matched. Our shortest-answer rule removes far fewer tokens than random removal. Either match or report tokens, or change the title word to "Cost-Accounted".
4. **Three new results weaken the premise that data repairs have room to work.** A capacity-rank-recipe map finds LoRA rank and recipe matter more than the corpus. A NeurIPS 2026 workshop paper finds seed changes moved results more than data edits on some benchmarks. A third submission finds the damage sits on the first word of the answer. None scoops the comparison. All three make the DIFFUSE outcome and the six-seed design easier to defend. Cite them.
5. **The side study (§3.6, §4.10, "refusal openings") is largely pre-empted.** "A Harmful Request Is Still a Request" (ICLR 2027 submission) puts the damage on the answer's opening token across 17 models. It also gives an "opening hold" loss that cuts harmful compliance from 22.6% to 0.9%. Reframe the side study as a replication, and decide whether the opening hold should be a reference.
6. **ICML 2027 call for papers: not published.** icml.cc lists only "2027 — South America". Planning dates come from a third-party tracker (paper about 22 Jan 2027 AoE). The last official rules (ICML 2026) allow 8 main-body pages. The draft's editor's note measures the body at 9.36 pages.

## 3. Entries (new work, 1 Aug – 16 Oct 2026, plus the July items that matter)

Each entry: what it does; closeness and the overlap; scoop or weaken; what to cite or add. "Abstract read" means I opened the abstract at its source.

### 1. Safety Risk of Fine-Tuning Data Is Not a Property of the Sample Alone (SABA)
- **Source.** Anonymous, ICLR 2027 submission, created 18 Sep 2026. https://openreview.net/forum?id=Jo6U0y2omr . Abstract read (search API); full text not opened.
- **What it does.** On benign fine-tuning, risk rankings computed at the start are "nearly uncorrelated" with rankings after two epochs. The same benign groups cause different damage from different model states. Gradient risk scores at the start are dominated by a component almost orthogonal to the safety direction. SABA re-scores each round and spreads a fixed exclusion budget over rounds. "Under the same training budget" it lowers ASR from 63.3% to 18.3% (CatQA) and from 53.1% to 14.4% (HEx-PHI), beats nineteen baselines, and holds on a second corpus and family.
- **Closeness: high** (removal repairs; attribution vs representation filters; "same training budget").
- **Overlap.** Our attribution and LARF-style arms score rows once, at the untuned model, then retrain. SABA says this is the weak design. It also compares many filters at one budget.
- **Scoop / weaken.** Does not scoop: removal only (no additions, no model-level references in the abstract; seeds and over-refusal not stated). **Weakens** the removal arms' design and gives reviewers a ready objection to Gate C ("one-shot gradient scores track a non-safety component").
- **Cite / add.** Cite in §2 (removal repairs) and §6. State in §3/§4 that we score once because a data owner decides before training; name re-scoring as out of scope. In Gate C, report how much of the attribution score's variance lies along a safety direction (one extra analysis, no new runs).

### 2. Follow the Gradient: Tracing Safety Degradation Back to Training Data
- **Source.** Anonymous, ICLR 2027 submission, created 19 Sep 2026. https://openreview.net/forum?id=aY4nMxI86R . Abstract read.
- **What it does.** A cheaper low-rank gradient attribution (back-propagation through the last four blocks only). Scores Tulu 3 rows against unsafe responses and removes the top 20% ("SafeTulu"). Fine-tuning on SafeTulu lowers GCG attack success by up to 22% on Qwen, Llama and OLMo, beats an LLM-as-judge filter, and mostly keeps capability. On emergent misalignment, AUC up to 0.865.
- **Closeness: high** (our attribution-removal arm; OLMo is one of its families).
- **Scoop / weaken.** Does not scoop the comparison: no addition repairs, no model-level references, no random-removal control or seeds in the abstract. It adds to the evidence that "attribution removal works" is established (with Pan et al., DATE-LM, Paulo et al.), so the paper must not present the attribution arm as new.
- **Cite / add.** Cite in §2 "Removal repairs". Its last-four-blocks trick is a cheaper attribution variant; mention it in §4.7 cost discussion as a lower-cost option we did not test.

### 3. A Harmful Request Is Still a Request: Benign Fine-Tuning Rewrites How Aligned Models Begin to Answer
- **Source.** Anonymous, ICLR 2027 submission, created 19 Sep 2026. https://openreview.net/forum?id=HdUlQV6dde . Abstract read.
- **What it does.** Benign fine-tuning weakens refusal in all 17 open models tested. The damage sits on the first word of the answer, computed at the last chat-template token ("the opening"). The model's judgement of harm survives: forced to begin with "I", a fine-tuned model still refuses harmful requests far more than harmless ones. An "opening hold" loss keeps the opening's internal state at the aligned value during fine-tuning and cuts harmful compliance from 22.6% to 0.9% over six models; freezing "safety layers" does not. The opening also gives an early warning in one forward pass.
- **Closeness: high** for the side study (§3.6, §4.10 "refusal openings against harmful content") and for the reference set (B+Qi is also a first-token constraint).
- **Scoop / weaken.** **Largely scoops the side study's question.** Does not scoop the repair comparison. It also notes that "two datasets learned equally well can have very different effects on safety", which supports asking a data question.
- **Cite / add.** Cite in §2 (shallow alignment, next to Qi et al. 2025) and in §3.6/§4.10. Reframe the side study as a check of this account on OLMo-2 and Llama-3. Decide whether to add the opening hold as a sixth reference; if not, say why (e.g., it changes the objective, like B+Qi, and B+Qi already covers that class).

### 4. When Does In-House Adaptation Cost Safety? A Capacity-Rank-Recipe Map
- **Source.** Anonymous, ICLR 2027 submission, created 19 Sep 2026. https://openreview.net/forum?id=tjNgRr5kox . Abstract read.
- **What it does.** Qwen2.5 0.5B–7B, 29 configurations (28 at three seeds), multiple-comparison correction. LoRA rank decides whether safety survives: under a conservative recipe nothing below rank 64 leaves a ±0.05 band; rank-128 cells at 1.5B–7B lose 0.15–0.22. An aggressive recipe collapses every scale. "The corpus is the weaker lever": a safety-neutral corpus costs most of the same loss. The collapse disappears when the adaptation safety prompt is present at evaluation.
- **Closeness: high** (premise of data repair; Gate B; recipe dependence; the safety-prompt reference; seeds and multiplicity).
- **Scoop / weaken.** Does not scoop. **Weakens the premise** that changing the data is the main lever, and supports the DIFFUSE branch. It also shows protocol dependence for a safety prompt.
- **Cite / add.** Cite in §1 next to Kim et al. (2025) and in §6. State the LoRA rank (r64 in the main recipe) as a scope condition, and report the rank-16 check (already in §4.10) against this map.

### 5. Safety Erosion from Benign Narrow Fine-Tuning
- **Source.** Anonymous, AI4GOOD Workshop @ NeurIPS 2026, poster. https://openreview.net/forum?id=gn1r0ZRngD . Abstract read.
- **What it does.** Fine-tunes GPT-4.1, Qwen3.5-27B and Llama-3.3-70B on AI-drafted, human-edited customer-service replies. Safety-relevant behaviour moves in every fine-tune; capability holds. Three edited variants of the data (flatter tone, no sales tactics, other product) "each changed little". A low-openness personality dataset erodes as much or more. A self-distilled dataset is the only arm at or near baseline. Retraining the same data with a different seed moved results more than any data change on some benchmarks.
- **Closeness: high** (localization; seed replication; the self-generated arm).
- **Scoop / weaken.** Does not scoop (no repair comparison, no cost). **Weakens** the hope that data edits help, and directly supports six seeds.
- **Cite / add.** Cite in §1 (seed noise) and §2 (addition repairs: self-distilled data). Mark it as a workshop paper.

### 6. Token Mass, Not Geometry: A Confound in Embedding-Based Data Selection for Language Model Pretraining
- **Source.** Anonymous, ICLR 2027 submission, created 17 Sep 2026. https://openreview.net/forum?id=q9STd7ZSfO . Abstract read.
- **What it does.** Under a fixed *document* budget, near-centroid selection beat random by a small, seed-stable margin, because it kept longer documents and so more tokens. With a matched *token* budget, the gain vanished. Three seeds, a random baseline, matched compute.
- **Closeness: high on method** (not on safety).
- **Scoop / weaken.** **Weakens "compute-matched".** Our removals are matched in rows (600), not tokens. The shortest-answer rule removes the fewest tokens; DataShield (Zhang et al., 2026b) reports that eroding responses tend to be longer, so attribution and representation picks may remove more tokens than random. The training dose then differs by arm.
- **Cite / add.** Cite in §4.3 and §4.7. Report tokens removed and optimizer steps per arm. Either add a token-matched random-removal control or enter tokens removed as a covariate in the contrasts. Otherwise change "Compute-Matched" in the title (see §5).

### 7. When Safety Becomes An Outlier: Understanding the Retention of LLM Safety Behaviors
- **Source.** Binchi Zhang, Hadi Abdullah, Yiwei Cai, Jundong Li. NeurIPS 2026, poster. https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/poster/151380 (OpenReview id b28qPD14np). Abstract read (NeurIPS page). No arXiv version found by title.
- **What it does.** Formulaic refusal strings behave like "outlier modes": learned and forgotten like arbitrary constant strings. Prompt-grounded, natural responses are retained better under later fine-tuning. Natural safety data close to the model's own output distribution (perplexity as proxy) improves safety and its retention.
- **Closeness: high** for the addition arms (random vs selected vs self-generated refusals).
- **Scoop / weaken.** Does not scoop. It **predicts** that the self-generated arm beats the random and DataRx-style arms, so a win for that arm is less surprising.
- **Cite / add.** Cite in §2 (addition repairs) and in the pre-registered expectations. Record the perplexity, under the untuned model, of each arm's 48 refusal examples (cheap; no new runs).

### 8. Safety at One Shot: Patching Fine-Tuned LLMs with A Single Instance (earlier, missing)
- **Source.** Jiawen Zhang et al., arXiv 2601.01887 (5 Jan 2026). https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.01887 . Abstract read (arXiv). Venue ICLR 2026 per the project's 12 Oct note, which cites https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/3d7cb780442f0d17c844dbe34997fab7-Abstract-Conference.html ; that page was not opened this session.
- **What it does.** Safety is "fully recovered with only a single safety example" after fine-tuning, without utility loss, across five aligned models; a low-rank safety gradient explains it.
- **Closeness: high** (an addition-type repair at almost no cost). Flagged in `research/review/2026-10-11-novelty-rigor-update.md` (12 Oct addendum) but **not in the draft**.
- **Scoop / weaken.** Does not scoop. **Weakens** the case for paid diagnosis if a one-example patch works on our setting.
- **Cite / add.** Cite in §2. Either add it as a reference (it changes the model after training, like Safe LoRA) or explain its exclusion before stage 1.

### 9. Clean Data, Unsafe Model: Certified Safety Curation for LLM Fine-Tuning
- **Source.** Anonymous, ICLR 2027 submission, created 19 Sep 2026. https://openreview.net/forum?id=ph3FlvaE9a . Abstract read.
- **What it does.** A distribution-free certificate on the harmful fraction of a filtered set. Judge filters discard safe demonstrations on harmful prompts; at 0.5B a judge-selected set certified at most 10% harmful trains a *more* harmful model than the pool; on an aligned 8B model a judge asked SAFT's question keeps a set more harmful than its pool. "Spectral and bilevel filter scores refuse under the same certificate."
- **Closeness: medium** (removal filters can remove protective rows).
- **Cite / add.** Cite in §2. Check, from the menus already built, whether any removal arm removes Dolly rows whose answers decline or caution.

### 10. Multilingual Safety Signals Are Multi-Layered: Filtering Safety-Degrading Data for Safer LLMs (MMSAFE)
- **Source.** Jiakun Li et al., arXiv 2609.22144 (26 Aug 2026). https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22144 . Abstract read.
- **What it does.** Safety-sensitive layers are only partly shared across languages; a multi-layer filter cuts the harmful-response ratio by 60% against random filtering and beats the strongest single-layer baseline.
- **Closeness: medium** (our representation arm is LARF-style, single-layer).
- **Cite / add.** Cite in §2 next to LARF. Say the LARF-style arm is a single-layer filter and multi-layer variants exist.

### 11. DataShield: Uncovering Risky Fine-Tuning Data Across LLMs Through Consensus Subspace Alignment
- **Source.** Zefeng Wu et al., arXiv 2607.15081 (16 Jul 2026). https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15081 . Abstract read.
- **What it does.** Scores samples and response segments against consensus safe/unsafe subspaces from several aligned LLMs; filtering lowers ASR by 14.6%, segment masking by 32.3%.
- **Closeness: medium.** **Name collision:** the draft's "DataShield (Zhang et al., 2026b), arXiv:2606.00160" is a *different* paper by Junbo Zhang et al.
- **Cite / add.** Keep author-year keys everywhere; add Wu et al. as a separate entry; never write "DataShield" alone.

### 12. Safety Offset Learning: When Benign Fine-Tuning Misaligns LLMs (SafeOff)
- **Source.** Anonymous, ICLR 2027 submission, created 17 Sep 2026. https://openreview.net/forum?id=ly2a5AxXAH . Abstract read.
- **What it does.** Benign examples that the aligned model would first refuse, paired with harmless answers, teach a refusal-to-compliance shift that transfers to harmful requests. SafeOff is a benign rewrite dataset built this way.
- **Closeness: medium** (which benign rows erode; a candidate cheap scorer).
- **Cite / add.** Cite in §2. In Gate C, "the untuned model refuses this Dolly prompt" is a nearly free scorer to report beside the length rule (analysis only).

### 13. The Unequal Influence of Bad Advice: Using Training Data Attribution to Modulate Emergent Misalignment
- **Source.** Gonçalo Paulo et al., arXiv 2609.37914 (29 Sep 2026); also ICLR 2027 submission (https://openreview.net/forum?id=iYCfBOgxYJ). Abstract read.
- **What it does.** Attribution scores, validated by retraining, can enhance or weaken emergent misalignment by filtering; a black-box harmfulness score also finds consequential examples; same-model scores filter best.
- **Closeness: medium.** Known to the project (11 Oct note) but **not in the draft**.
- **Cite / add.** Cite in §2 "Removal repairs".

### 14. Anchored but Not Protected: When Refusal Erodes During Fine-Tuning, and Whether Holding One Direction Stops It
- **Source.** Anonymous, ICLR 2027 submission, created 19 Sep 2026. https://openreview.net/forum?id=7l9gH9h9aZ . Abstract read.
- **What it does.** LLaMA-3-8B-Instruct and Qwen2-7B-Instruct on ordinary instruction data. 52 of 517 optimizer steps carry 89–101% of epoch-1 displacement. Restoring the refusal coordinate at inference recovers most of the gap. "Three scorers disagree by up to 70 points on identical responses"; one rubric built two ways changes a pre-registered effect from 3.6 to 2.2 points.
- **Closeness: medium** (measurement; a refusal-direction defence; pre-registration).
- **Cite / add.** Cite in §4.2 and §6 to support two judges and the blind read; note that judge choice can decide a verdict.

### 15. Are Coreset Selection Methods Worth Their Cost?
- **Source.** Yangze Liu et al., arXiv 2609.22894 (19 Sep 2026); also ICLR 2027 submission (https://openreview.net/forum?id=F237KyyMev). Abstract read.
- **What it does.** End-to-end wall-clock benchmark (11 selectors, 5 fractions, 3 seeds, 1,674 runs, CIFAR-10 to ImageNet-1K). No budget anchor is won by a sophisticated selector. Selection cost is dominated by a fixed full-dataset scan and does not amortize.
- **Closeness: medium on cost**, none on safety.
- **Cite / add.** Cite in §2 "Attribution, its cost" beside Yin & Rush (2025).

### 16. HyperSafe: Inference-Time Safety Recovery for Fine-Tuned Language Models
- **Source.** Aznaur Aliev et al., arXiv 2607.11475 (13 Jul 2026); also ICLR 2027 submission (https://openreview.net/forum?id=foJOTjtyT2). Abstract read.
- **What it does.** A hypernetwork builds a side safety classifier per fine-tuned checkpoint from activation fingerprints; targets benign-fine-tuning erosion; harmful responses 19–31% → below 1% on Qwen2-7B and LLaMA-3-8B, second judge, about 3.4% extra inference FLOPs.
- **Closeness: medium** (a model-level repair that changes no data).
- **Cite / add.** Cite in §2 "model-level repairs" as a class we do not include (needs a per-family hypernetwork).

### 17. Restoring LLM Safety via Sparse Weight Interpolation (SWI)
- **Source.** Anonymous, ICLR 2027 submission, created 16 Sep 2026. https://openreview.net/forum?id=R5IVtCQliF . Abstract read.
- **What it does.** Post-hoc, row-level masked interpolation toward the aligned model; restores safety with "as few as 10 safety demonstrations" and keeps benign skill.
- **Closeness: medium** (like our down-scaled-adapter reference, but learned).
- **Cite / add.** Cite in §2 next to Safe LoRA and adapter scaling.

### 18. Can Data Attribution Filter Out Subliminal Learning? Not Reliably
- **Source.** Moritz Weckbecker et al., arXiv 2609.20027 (17 Sep 2026). https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20027 . Abstract read.

- **Closeness: medium** (negative evidence on attribution filtering).

### 19. Safety Measurements for Fine-tuned LLMs Should be Grounded in Capability (June; missing)
- **Source.** Krishnapriya Vishnubhotla et al., arXiv 2606.03648 (2 Jun 2026). https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03648 . Abstract read.
- **What it does.** Fine-tuned models can give incoherent answers to safety prompts; automated judges are unreliable on them; conclusions about fine-tuning effects change with the benchmark and the evaluator. Argues for comparing mitigations at a fixed capability goal.

## 5. Does any new work change the novelty claim? Exact wording changes

**Claim tested:** "No prior work compares data fixes of several types for benign-fine-tuning erosion at matched compute with side-effect limits and seed replication."

**Verdict: the claim survives only with tighter qualifiers.** Every clause alone is now covered somewhere:
- "data fixes of several types": DualSelect (six selectors + one safety mix), DataRx (six addition selections), SABA (a filter against nineteen baselines).
- "seed replication": DualSelect (3 seeds), the capability-rank-recipe map (3 seeds), the coreset cost benchmark (3 seeds), Paramkusham (3 seeds).
- "side-effect limits": DualSelect (false-refusal rate), DataRx (XSTest in one analysis).
- "matched compute": SABA ("same training budget"), DualSelect (matched data budget, selection cost reported), coreset cost benchmark.

What I did **not** find anywhere: (a) purely benign data, (b) removal *and* addition repairs in one design, including attribution vs representation vs a free length rule, (c) full cost of every arm, scoring included, in one unit, (d) over-refusal *and* task loss as pre-set limits, (e) better/equivalent/worse/inconclusive verdicts with equivalence bounds and Holm correction over six seeds, (f) model-level references a data repair must beat, and (g) pre-registered gates. Absence from my search is not proof of novelty.

The word **"matched compute"** is the risk. The design does not equalize compute across arms; it measures it. Removals are row-matched, not token-matched.

**ICML 2026 rules (official for 2026; the best guide until 2027's call):**
- Deadlines: abstract 23 Jan 2026 AoE; full paper 28 Jan 2026 AoE; no extensions.
- **Page limit: 8 pages** main body, single file. References, the Impact Statement and appendices do not count and are unlimited. Camera-ready gets one extra page. **The draft's body measures 9.36 pages** (editor's note, revision 5e), so it is about 1.4 pages over.
- **Impact Statement: required**, as a separate section at the end, before references, not counted. A standard sentence ("This paper presents work whose goal is to advance the field of machine learning…") is allowed for well-established impacts.
- **Dual submission:** papers identical or substantially similar to published work, or under review elsewhere in parallel, are not allowed. Work at workshops without published proceedings is allowed.
- **Preprints:** arXiv posting is allowed. Do not advertise the work as an ICML submission during review. If a non-anonymized version is posted, the submission must not refer to it.
- Also: reciprocal reviewing (at least one author per submission must review), LLM-use policy (authors responsible; no prompt injection), double-blind anonymity.
